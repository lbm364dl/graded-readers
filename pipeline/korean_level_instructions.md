# Korean editions at TOPIK 2–6

The explicit target supplied with this run governs its planning, prose,
annotation and review. Level 1 wording in the inherited pilot instructions
describes that pilot, not the target of this edition. Use the supplied target
goals and the cumulative NIKL curriculum through that level. The catalog is a
curriculum baseline, not an exhaustive exam whitelist.

Each edition is a real adaptation of the original. Choose its source coverage
and coherent stopping point for this learner level. Do not merely relabel the
beginner text, add difficult synonyms, or force longer chapters at higher levels.
Richer vocabulary, syntax and detail should serve natural storytelling and the
source. Do not manufacture complexity to hit a minimum grammar level.

Use a consistent modern narrative register appropriate to the target. Formal
polite narration is suitable for the beginner pilot; higher editions may use
natural written narrative endings. Dictionary forms remain neutral, and observed
forms and complete transformation meanings belong to passage occurrences.

Retrieve source vocabulary candidates for the actual words and productive
prerequisites under review rather than repeatedly transmitting the full 10,635
word catalog. Candidate retrieval never proves sense, identity, or grade.
Explicit bindings and independent whole-chapter review still gate publication.

A few higher-level or unlisted grammar patterns may be useful. Preserve their
real grades (or honest unlisted status), explain their optional role for this
target, and judge frequency, variety, complexity and dependence across the whole
chapter. No fixed grammar quota. Optional labels cannot excuse advanced overload.
Sentence help is selected by difficulty for this target, not by sentence length
alone. Reuse approved dictionary identities and definitions across editions.
